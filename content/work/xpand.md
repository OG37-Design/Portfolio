## Assumptions (slide 013)

## Testing Our Assumptions (slides 014–018)
![Slide 14](/work/xpand/014.JPG)
![Slide 15](/work/xpand/015.JPG)
![Slide 16](/work/xpand/016.JPG)
![Slide 17](/work/xpand/017.JPG)
![Slide 18](/work/xpand/018.JPG)
Multicultural Questionnaire: Expertise, 2019 — 150 participants (100 China, 50 US).
- 72% of all participants think they have or might have an expertise.
- For the 19% who think they might: "mostly lack of confidence stops them from declaring their expertise."
- For the 28% who don't think they have an expertise: 73% of them are willing to learn from others.
- 96% of all participants say there's a skill they want to master. 43% say lack of time stops them from learning, others mention laziness and money.

This directly validates the four assumptions and sets up the time/money design response below.

## Design Rationale: Time as Currency (slide 024)
![Slide 24](/work/xpand/024.png)
> "[Time & Money] where the most dominant variers to engaging in the proses of learning a new skill. This was even though the majority of people had a clear focus on the area they wished to engage in. We challeged our selves to look at these variers in a different lense. One where they became the asset and not the aspect of life that held users back. We turned time into the currency that would enable users to participate in Xpand. Participants would trade their time in exchange for engaging in a learning experience while the contributors would gain in app currency."

(Likely missing the word "were" after the heading; original typos "variers"/"proses"/"challeged"/"lense" preserved — meaning is clear: time and money were the two biggest barriers to learning a new skill, so Xpand reframes time itself as the in-app currency.)
